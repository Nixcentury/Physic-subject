Gravitation and the Sun–Earth–Moon System

Covers Test Paper Q1–Q4 · Gravitational law, G, calculation, inverse-square relationship
Word Bank
attractivemassessquaredistance6.67 × 10⁻¹¹one ninth2.04 × 10²⁰ N

Newton’s law of universal gravitation states that gravity is an  attractive force between objects. The force is directly proportional to the product of their  masses and inversely proportional to the  square of the  distance between them. The universal gravitational constant is  6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻².

\[\displaystyle F=\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^2}\]
Equation meaning
\(F\propto m_1m_2\)   and   \(F\propto \dfrac{1}{r^2}\)
\(G=6.67\times10^{-11}\;\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}\)

For the Earth–Moon values used in the test paper, the gravitational force is approximately  2.04 × 10²⁰ N. If the distance becomes three times larger while the masses remain unchanged, the force becomes  one ninth of its original value.

Quick ratio check
If \(r\to 2r\), then \(F\to \dfrac{F}{4}\).
If \(r\to 3r\), then \(F\to \dfrac{F}{9}\).
If \(m_1\to 2m_1\), then \(F\to 2F\) when the other quantities stay the same.

Earth’s Axis, Day–Night, Seasons, Solstice and Equinox

Covers Test Paper Q5–Q10 · Axial tilt, rotation, revolution, seasons, solstice, equinox
Word Bank
23.5°rotationrevolutionwintersummersolsticeequinoxequatorlongest

Earth rotates on an imaginary axis that passes through the North Pole, the centre of Earth and the South Pole. The axis is tilted by about  23.5°. Day and night are caused by Earth’s  rotation. The seasons are caused by the combined effects of the axial tilt and Earth’s  revolution around the Sun.

When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, it receives less sunlight and experiences  winter, while the Southern Hemisphere receives more sunlight and experiences  summer. Around June in the Northern Hemisphere, the summer  solstice marks the beginning of summer and gives the  longest daytime. An  equinox occurs when the Sun is directly above the  equator, and daytime and nighttime are approximately equal in length.

Axial tilt: \(\theta\approx 23.5^{\circ}\)
Test Paper Q5 reminder: Axial tilt helps cause the seasons, but it does not cause the phases of the Moon. Moon phases are linked to the Moon’s revolution around Earth and the changing lit area we see.

The Moon: Phases, Revolution, Moonrise and Moonset

Covers Test Paper Q11–Q12 + Topic 8.2 review · Moon phases and approximate rise/highest/set times
Word Bank
reflectsrevolves12°Full Mooneast50 minutes29.5 days

The Moon does not produce its own visible light; it  reflects light from the Sun. As the Moon  revolves around Earth, we see different amounts of its sunlit surface. These changing appearances are called the phases of the Moon. When the Moon is on the opposite side of Earth from the Sun, the phase is a  Full Moon.

A complete phase cycle takes about  29.5 days. As the Moon moves about  12° toward the  east each day, moonrise occurs about  50 minutes later from one day to the next.

Tides: High Tide, Low Tide, Spring Tide and Neap Tide

Covers Test Paper Q13–Q14 + Topic 8.2 review · Gravity, Earth’s rotation and tidal patterns
Word Bank
MoonSunrotationtwospring tideneap tidelineright angles

Tides are the alternate rising and falling of ocean water. In this topic, the gravitational force of the  Moon is the main cause described, while the gravitational force of the  Sun also affects tides. Earth’s  rotation makes a place pass through high-water and low-water regions. In the simplified model, a place normally experiences about  two high tides and two low tides each day.

A  spring tide has very high high tides and very low low tides. It occurs when the Earth, Moon and Sun are in a  line. A  neap tide has the least difference between high and low tides and occurs when the Sun and Moon are at  right angles to Earth.

Timing memory aid: High → about 6 h → Low → about 6 h → High → about 6 h → Low. This is a simplified learning model; real local tide times vary and are not fixed at the same clock time every day.

Space Technology and Space Exploration

Covers Test Paper Q15–Q20 + Topic 8.3 · Satellites, GNSS, EOS, probes, telescopes, shuttle, ISS
Word Bank
satellitesspace probestelescopesGNSSpositioningEarth-observation satellitesreusableISSweatherforest firesoil spills

Modern space exploration uses man-made  satellites,  space probes and  telescopes to collect information about space. The Global Navigation Satellite System, or  GNSS, uses a group of satellites to transmit  positioning and timing data to receivers, which then determine locations and support navigation.

 Earth-observation satellites (EOS) observe Earth from space. They can help predict the  weather, detect  forest fires and  oil spills, and reduce disaster risks. Communication satellites relay voice, video and data.

The space shuttle was important because it was described in the source as a  reusable spacecraft. The International Space Station, or  ISS, is a large spacecraft orbiting Earth where astronauts can live, work and carry out research.

Final Word Bank

Gravity between two objects depends on their  masses and decreases with  r². Earth’s axis is tilted by about  23.5°. Earth’s  rotation causes day and night, while axial tilt together with Earth’s  revolution causes seasons. During an equinox, the Sun is directly above the  equator.

When the Moon is opposite the Sun with Earth between them, we see a  Full Moon. Moonrise becomes about  50 minutes later each day. When the Sun, Earth and Moon are aligned, a  spring tide occurs. In space technology,  GNSS supports positioning and navigation, while  EOS observes Earth for weather and hazards. The space shuttle is remembered as  reusable, and the ISS provides a place for astronauts to live, work and carry out  research.
